Uncertainty Quantification & Embedded AI

Bosch Research, Renningen & Sunnyvale 09.2020 — 06.2024
  • On-device learning
  • NAS
  • Uncertainty distillation

A model that is accurate in a notebook is not necessarily a model you can ship on a sensor. I built a multi-objective, hardware-aware architecture search that trades accuracy, uncertainty quality, and hardware cost against each other, using ensemble pruning and uncertainty distillation to collapse an ensemble into one deployable network that still knows when it is wrong.

In Silicon Valley I extended this to learning that happens on the device itself: human activity recognition from multivariate time series, adapting to the user without the data ever leaving the sensor.

Quantum machine learning

University of Stuttgart, IAAS 05.2022 — 11.2022
  • QNNs
  • Entanglement
  • Learning theory

How few training samples does a quantum neural network really need? We extended the Quantum No-Free-Lunch theorem to training sets with varying degrees of entanglement, and showed what happens to the expected risk when samples are orthogonal or linearly dependent. The bounds were validated by simulating QNN training on randomly generated 6-qubit target unitaries.

On reducing the amount of samples required for training of QNNs: constraints on the linear structure of the training data Quantum Machine Intelligence 7, 101 (2025) — open access

Diabetic retinopathy detection

University of Stuttgart, Institute for AI 04.2022 - 09.2022
  • Explainable AI
  • Medical imaging
  • Transfer learning

Developed a CNN for explainable diabetic retinopathy detection (XAI) that grades retinal photographs while letting a clinician see why it decided what it decided. Applied state-of-the-art feature extraction techniques, transfer learning, and explainability approaches, on the premise that in medicine an unexplained prediction is not a usable one.
